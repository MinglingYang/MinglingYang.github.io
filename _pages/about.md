I am Mingling (Mona) Yang, a Senior Research Data Analyst at the Johns Hopkins University School of Medicine and the Center for Global Non-Communicable Disease Research and Training. I hold an M.S.E. in Applied Mathematics and Statistics from Johns Hopkins University and a B.S. in Statistics from the University of South Carolina.

My research centers on applying advanced biostatistical and geospatial methods to global health and environmental epidemiology. I have worked extensively on large multinational studies, including the Household Air Pollution Intervention Network (HAPIN) and the Global Excellence in COPD Outcomes (GECo) study, under the mentorship of Dr. William Checkley and Dr. Laura Nicolaou. My recent work focuses on developing calibration models for low-cost air quality sensors using geostatistics and machine learning to improve exposure assessment in low- and middle-income countries.

Broadly, my research interests lie at the intersection of environmental exposure assessment, causal inference, and longitudinal data analysis—particularly how statistical innovation can advance our understanding of respiratory and child health outcomes in resource-limited settings.

I am currently preparing for Ph.D. study in Epidemiology beginning in Fall 2026, where I aim to develop novel statistical frameworks for exposure-response modeling and policy evaluation in global environmental health.
